## Decisions made (link ADRs)

- **OQ-B2-1** — a `past_due` customer keeps *full* access through Stripe's retry schedule; access
  stops at `unpaid`. We keep no timer of our own: Stripe owns the retry schedule and moving to
  `unpaid` is its signal that the schedule is exhausted, so a parallel clock would only drift.
- **OQ-B2-2** — plan changes go through the Stripe Billing Portal; upgrades prorate immediately,
  downgrades take effect at period end. No proration UI of our own.
- **OQ-B2-3** — left open, deliberately. A genuine double subscription stays a manual decision;
  the incident queue exists to make manual handling tolerable.
- No ADR was needed — these are product/policy calls recorded in the B2 spec, not architecture
  changes. The provider seam, module boundaries, and session-auth decisions all held unchanged.

## What I got wrong, worth remembering

- Shipped a `DataError` to the owner's browser: `settle` writes `settle:<session_id>` into a
  `varchar(64)` column, and Stripe *session* ids are ~66-70 chars where *event* ids are ~28. Nine
  tests exercised that exact path and all passed, because they used `cs_test_1` as the session id.
  **Fake identifiers in tests should be the length of the real thing**, not a readable stub — a
  short stand-in silently removes the constraint being tested.
- The same review that found seven bugs in someone else's code produced one in mine within the
  hour. Worth noting that the audit mindset did not automatically transfer to the writing.

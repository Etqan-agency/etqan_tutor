## Goal

Turn a `TeacherAssignment` into lessons that happen at a time, and make the number the parent
paid for mean something.

C1 matched a student to a teacher and stopped there: the pair exists, and nothing is scheduled.
`SubscriptionPlan.sessions_per_cycle` has existed since B1 and has never been read — B1's spec
says so in as many words, deferring "session-pool consumption / quotas" to this slice. So a
parent can buy four sessions a month and receive none, and nothing in the system notices.

C2 closes both gaps: a student claims a **weekly slot** with their teacher, sessions are
generated from it inside the billing cycle up to quota, and either side can cancel one.

**Out of scope, stated up front so the plan does not drift:** video rooms and join links (C3),
session reports and ratings (B3), trials, and moving a single occurrence to a different time.

## Decisions taken

Settled during brainstorming, recorded so the plan does not relitigate them:

1. **A student claims a recurring weekly slot**, not one session at a time. 1-on-1 tuition is a
   standing appointment; making a weekly student rebook four times a month is a chore the model
   should absorb, and it leaves nothing holding their usual time.
2. **A scheduled session consumes quota; a timely cancellation refunds it.** Not "only completed
   sessions count" — that cannot be checked at booking time, and lets a student hold ten slots
   against a four-session plan.
3. **24 hours is timely, and a teacher cancelling always refunds** regardless of notice. The
   asymmetry is deliberate: a student should not pay for their teacher's change of plans.
4. **Sessions are rows, generated by a job, one billing cycle at a time.** Not implied
   occurrences: a session row is what C3 hangs a join link on and B3 hangs a report on, so the
   row has to exist on an ordinary week, not only on a deviating one.

## Data model delta

All new models live in `scheduling`.

```text
scheduling.RecurringSlot                     (new)
    assignment      FK TeacherAssignment
    weekday         PositiveSmallIntegerField    # 0=Sat .. 6=Fri, matching WeeklyAvailability
    start_time      TimeField                    # UTC
    status          ACTIVE | ENDED
    created_at / ended_at
    partial-unique (assignment) WHERE status = ACTIVE

scheduling.Session                           (new)
    assignment      FK TeacherAssignment
    slot            FK RecurringSlot
    starts_at       DateTimeField                 # UTC instant
    duration_minutes PositiveSmallIntegerField    # 60 in C2
    status          SCHEDULED | COMPLETED | CANCELLED
    cancelled_by    FK AUTH_USER_MODEL, null=True
    cancelled_at    DateTimeField, null=True
    cycle_end       DateTimeField                 # the billing period this counts against
    consumes_quota  BooleanField default True
    created_at
    unique (assignment, starts_at)
```

**Slot times are stored in UTC, not as a local wall-clock.** `WeeklyAvailability` stores local
times and interprets them in the owner's timezone, which is right for "when am I free" — one
person, one zone. A slot belongs to *two* people in *two* zones, so a local time has no single
meaning: a student who travels would silently move their teacher's lesson. The claim is resolved
to UTC once, when it is made, and stays there.

**`consumes_quota` is a stored field, not a status inference.** A session cancelled thirty hours
ahead and one cancelled thirty minutes ahead are both `CANCELLED`, and only the first returns the
unit. Deriving that at read time would mean recomputing a cancellation-time comparison on every
quota query, against a rule that may change; storing it makes the count a filter and leaves an
audit trail of *why* a unit came back.

**A refunded unit is re-materialised only if the slot has another occurrence left in the cycle,
and otherwise goes unused.** This is the honest consequence of weekly slots plus a per-cycle
quota, and it is worth stating rather than discovering: a student on Saturdays with four
Saturdays in the month who cancels one in good time gets the unit back, but there is no fifth
Saturday to spend it on. Giving them a one-off session at some other time would need a
booking flow C2 does not have, and carrying it into next cycle would make `sessions_per_cycle`
mean something other than what it says. So `Session.slot` is **not nullable** — every session in
C2 comes from a slot — and OQ-C2-4 records the rebook question rather than half-answering it here.

**One active slot per assignment.** A student having two weekly lessons with the same teacher in
the same subject is not a case C2 serves, and the partial index says so rather than leaving it
ambiguous. Ending a slot and claiming another is the supported path.

## Quota

`sessions_per_cycle` is **per-student on an Individual plan and per-child on a Family plan** —
B1 already defines it that way, so there is no pool to allocate across siblings and C2 inherits
the answer rather than inventing one.

Consumed this cycle = sessions for that student where `cycle_end` is the current period's end
and `consumes_quota` is true, in any status. Remaining = `sessions_per_cycle` − consumed.

**The cycle is keyed by its END, not a start-and-end pair, and that is deliberate.** `Subscription`
mirrors `current_period_end` from the provider and has no `current_period_start`; keying on the
start would mean adding a field, mirroring it, and backfilling every existing row — a billing
migration in service of a scheduling counter. The end alone identifies a cycle uniquely, and it is
also the only bound generation needs: it fills forward from today to the period end. So `billing`
gains one service — `get_session_allowance(user) -> (limit, cycle_end)` — reading the plan's
`sessions_per_cycle` and the live subscription's `current_period_end`. It belongs in `billing` because the
plan and the period are billing's data; `scheduling` asking for them is the same direction as the
entitlement call it already makes. **No new module dependency and no ADR:** `scheduling → billing`
is already open (ADR-0033), through `billing.services` only.

## Generation

A **daily** Celery beat task ensures each active assignment's current cycle is materialised: for
every `ACTIVE` `RecurringSlot`, walk the weeks of the student's current billing period and create
a `SCHEDULED` session per occurrence, stopping at the quota. Idempotent — the
`unique (assignment, starts_at)` constraint is the backstop, and the task filters existing rows
rather than relying on it.

**Daily and idempotent rather than fired on the renewal webhook,** and that choice does the work
of a late-renewal rule: an unentitled student generates nothing, and the first run after they are
entitled again fills in whatever remains of the cycle. A webhook-triggered generator would need
its own catch-up path for every way a renewal can be late, out of order, or replayed — all of
which B1 already learned the hard way about Stripe event ordering.

Quota is enforced **by construction** here, not merely checked: nothing generates the fifth
session of a four-session cycle, so a quota bug cannot produce an unpaid lesson.

## Claiming a slot

The student or parent picks a weekly time from the **intersection of their availability and their
teacher's**. C1 already computes that overlap for matching (`matching.overlap_minutes`); C2 needs
the same computation to return the intervals rather than the total, so the shared helper is
extracted rather than reimplemented — the two must not be able to disagree about when two people
are both free.

Candidate times exclude anything that would double-book the teacher: **no two `ACTIVE` slots may
overlap for one teacher**, checked in the claiming transaction, not only in the candidate list a
client may be holding from a minute ago.

**Sessions are 60 minutes in C2**, as a named constant with a comment. Not a plan field and not
per-teacher: nothing in the product sells a different length yet, and the field is easy to add
when something does.

## API delta

All under `/api/v1/scheduling/` via `config.api_router` (ADR-0029).

| Method | Path | Who | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `GET` | `/slot-options/` | student / parent | weekly times where both are free and the teacher is not booked |
| `POST` | `/slots/` | student / parent | claim one → the created slot |
| `DELETE` | `/slots/{id}/` | student / parent | end it; future sessions are cancelled under the same 24h rule |
| `GET` | `/sessions/` | any party | role-aware upcoming sessions |
| `POST` | `/sessions/{id}/cancel/` | either side | applies the 24h rule and the teacher exemption |
| `POST` | `/sessions/{id}/complete/` | teacher | marks it done |
| `GET` | `/quota/` | student / parent | `{limit, used, remaining, cycle_end}` |

Cancelling is the rule's only home: the endpoint decides refund-or-not from the actor and the
notice, and nothing else in the system writes `consumes_quota`. A caller who is not a party to
the session gets a 403, not a 404 — the session's existence is not a secret from a participant
and not a question for anyone else. Payloads carry display names, never email addresses
(ADR-0023).

## Frontend

- **`/schedule`** — currently a placeholder scaffold, and this replaces it. Upcoming sessions for
  every role, each with its subject, the other party's name, and a cancel action where allowed.
- **Slot claiming**, for a student or parent who has an assignment and no active slot: the
  candidate weekly times, and what claiming one means.
- **The quota counter**, wherever the sessions live: used, remaining, and when the cycle resets.
  A student at zero must be told the number resets, and when — not merely refused.
- Cancellation copy states the consequence *before* the click: whether this cancellation returns
  the session to the pool. A student discovering after the fact that a late cancellation cost them
  a lesson is a support ticket the interface can prevent.
- en + ar, RTL verified, values from tokens, WCAG 2.2 AA.

## Test plan

TDD throughout (D3). Backend floor 97.2 with `precision = 1`; dashboard 92.5 / 88.5 / 85 / 92.5.
Raise them if this slice lifts coverage.

**Quota**
- Four scheduled sessions exhaust a four-session cycle; the generator creates no fifth.
- A cancellation ≥24h ahead returns the unit; a cancellation <24h ahead does not.
- A returned unit is re-materialised when the slot has a later occurrence inside the cycle, and
  is simply not used when it does not — asserted both ways, because the second is the surprising
  one and the one a support question will be about.
- A teacher cancellation refunds at any notice, including ten minutes before.
- Usage is counted per cycle: sessions carrying a previous period's `cycle_end` do not reduce
  this period's remaining.
- A Family plan gives each child their own allowance rather than a shared pool.

**Generation**
- Idempotent: running the task twice creates one set.
- An unentitled student generates nothing; entitlement arriving mid-cycle generates the remainder,
  not the whole cycle from its start.
- A slot ended mid-cycle stops generating, and its future sessions are cancelled and refunded.
- Sessions land on the right UTC instants across a timezone difference between the two parties.

**Claiming**
- A time where only one party is free is not offered and is refused if posted directly.
- A teacher cannot be double-booked, including when two students claim the same time
  concurrently — the loser gets a typed 409, as C1's accept race does.
- Claiming with no active assignment is a 403.

**Cancelling and completing**
- Each side can cancel; a stranger gets a 403.
- Completing is teacher-only, and a completed session cannot then be cancelled.

**Boundaries and plumbing** — `lint-imports` stays green with `billing.services` called and no
`billing.models` import; `test_api_versioning_is_enforced` still passes.

**Dashboard** — the schedule renders per role; the quota counter shows remaining and reset date;
cancel round-trips and states its consequence first; Arabic renders RTL.

**e2e** — a student claims a slot and sees generated sessions; a cancellation ≥24h ahead returns
the unit visibly; a teacher sees the same session on their own schedule. `seed_e2e_matching`
grows into the assignment C2 needs, or gains a sibling.

## Open questions

None blocking. Three to answer while building, none of which changes the data model:

- **OQ-C2-1.** Should a student be able to *move* a single occurrence rather than cancel and
  reclaim? Deliberately out of C2; it needs its own conflict and quota rules.
- **OQ-C2-2.** What happens to a `SCHEDULED` session when the subscription lapses mid-cycle?
  C2's answer is that it stands — the cycle was paid for — and generation simply stops. Worth
  revisiting if it turns out people lapse mid-cycle often.
- **OQ-C2-3.** Should the 24-hour window be a setting rather than a constant? A constant until a
  second value is actually wanted.
- **OQ-C2-4.** Should a refunded unit with no remaining occurrence be spendable on a one-off
  session at another time? That is the rebook flow C2 leaves out; whether it is worth building
  depends on how often people cancel early, which the `consumes_quota` audit trail will show.
